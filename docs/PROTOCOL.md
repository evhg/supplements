_2026-11-11. Four sticks (Morning, Pre-workout, Post-workout, Sleep), all launched together as a daily protocol.
v3.1 applies the Thai FDA rules for Wilder's registration route (beverage in sealed container, dry type, MOPH No. 356),
the cost check and the "two ingredients, one extra claim" pairings. The salt maths and calorie budget in `FORMULAS.md` still apply._

## 1. Fixed decisions

- **Four sticks, the maximum.** All launch together.
- **Every stick carries all 6 electrolytes:** sodium, chloride, potassium, magnesium, calcium, phosphate.
- **Creatine 2.5 g in one stick only (Post-workout), adults only, max 1 a day.** This keeps all four sticks on the drink-mix route. The other three are family-friendly: kids 4–12 take half a stick.
- **Morning is the GLP-1 stick.**
- **Pre-workout is caffeine-free.** No caffeine anywhere.
- **No L-theanine** (Wilder's ingredient; parents avoid it). **No melatonin** (a drug in Thailand).
- **Zero calorie, except one stick may be ~11 kcal.** That number is printed big and proud ("only 11 kcal").
- **Register like Wilder:** a dry drink mix under MOPH No. 356.

## 2. What Wilder's route allows (Thai FDA positive list, June 2023)

The Thai FDA publishes a **positive list for beverages in sealed containers** (except caffeine drinks): four lists of plants,
extracts, protein/carb/fibre/fat substances and "other substances". Anything on it at the listed dose needs no special
consultation; that's the "no hassle" path. Source: food.fda.moph.go.th, *BeverageList.pdf* (47 pages).

| Ingredient | On the list? | Limit | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Creatine monohydrate** | ✅ | **≤ 2.5 g/day** | **5 g doesn't fit this route** (see §5) |
| **Glycine** | ✅ | **≤ 100 mg/day** | Glyna-style 3 g glycine is impossible here; 100 mg is a token amount |
| **Inositol** | ✅ | **≤ 150 mg/day** | Huberman's 900 mg doesn't fit; 150 mg is a token amount |
| **GABA** | ✅ | limited per 100 ml by drink category (exact figure to confirm with the OEM) | ~100 mg looks workable |
| **Collagen peptides** | ✅ | ≤ 10 g/day | usable |
| **Chamomile** (German & Roman, flower) | ✅ | – | usable |
| **Ginger** (root; extract) | ✅ | extract ≤ 1 g/day as powder equivalent | usable |
| **Coconut, beetroot, spirulina, monk fruit** | ✅ | monk fruit with a daily cap | usable |
| **Vitamins & minerals** | ✅ under the Thai FDA **nutrification (fortification) guideline** | per that guideline | usable, but see vitamin D |
| **Taurine** | ❌ (only allowed in caffeine drinks, 0.13–1.5 g) | – | needs FDA consultation, and links us to M-150/Carabao energy drinks. **Drop.** |
| **L-citrulline, L-arginine, betaine, apigenin, BCAA, beta-alanine, carnitine** | ❌ | – | **not pre-approved**: possible only via a case-by-case FDA consultation (Wilder got L-theanine through, so it can be done, but it costs time) |

**Vitamin D: yes, the rules are tight.**
- **As a dietary supplement**, the cap is **15 µg (600 IU) a day** (MOPH No. 448, in force July 2024; before that 5 µg / 200 IU).
- **In a drink mix**, vitamins go through the fortification guideline and are tied to the Thai daily value: vitamin D's is only **5 µg (200 IU)**. Expect about that per serving at most.
- **So 1,000 IU isn't possible on Wilder's route.** I've dropped vitamin D as a selling point. The Morning stick can carry a small fortification dose if the OEM confirms.
- **Other supplement caps** worth knowing: vitamin B6 2 mg, vitamin K 80 µg, vitamin C 1,000 mg a day.

**Taurine isn't a stimulant.** It calms nerves rather than exciting them, and that's why it's sometimes studied for sleep. It only *seems* stimulating because it sits in energy drinks alongside caffeine. In Thailand it's also on the beverage list only for caffeine drinks, so we drop it rather than fight the M-150 association.

## 5. Creatine: decided 2.5 g per stick (2026-11-12)

**Decision: 2.5 g creatine monohydrate in Post-workout**, so all four sticks register as drink mixes on Wilder's route
(the Thai positive list allows creatine ≤ 2.5 g/day). Supplement registration is too heavy.

- **The pack must say max 1 stick a day.** The 2.5 g limit is per day, so "two sticks on training days" can't be on the label.
- **Why 2.5 g is still a good story:** Bryan Johnson's Longevity Mix uses 2.5–3 g a day. At 2.5 g daily, muscle stores still fill;
  it just takes a few weeks longer than at 5 g. Water retention is lower, which helps the women and GLP-1 angle.
  Pitch: *"Daily dose, no loading, no bloat."*
- **Second claim stays:** collagen peptides 2.5 g + vitamin C (joints and tendons), both on the beverage list.
- **The stick shrinks:** 2.5 g less powder, so ~8 g instead of ~10.5 g.

## 9. Reference: Ajinomoto Glyna (グリナ), the sleep stick Danny mentioned

- **Per 3.1 g stick:** glycine 3.0 g, citric acid, grapefruit flavour; 12.3 kcal. The "Sleep & Stress" version adds GABA 100 mg. ¥7,128/30 (≈ ฿1,500).
- **Evidence:** small Ajinomoto-run human trials (Yamadera 2007, n = 11, sleep-lab monitoring; Inagawa 2006 double-blind; Bannai 2012).
- **Fit for us:** good evidence, but at 3 g glycine it **can't be copied on Wilder's route** (glycine ≤ 100 mg/day). Our Sleep stick borrows the
  GABA + glycine pairing at legal levels. Glycine 3 g would need the supplement route.
