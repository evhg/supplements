# The Protocol: four sticks, v3 (draft)

_2026-11-11. Replaces the day system (PRODUCT_RANGE v2 / FORMULAS v1.x) as the range structure. Their per-gram
salt maths, calorie budget and OEM questions still apply._

## 1. The decisions this is built on

- **Four sticks, the maximum:** Morning, Pre-workout, Post-workout, Sleep. **All four launch together**, sold as a daily longevity protocol.
- **Creatine 5 g in one stick only (Post-workout), 0 in the rest.**
- The three creatine-free sticks are **family-friendly**: adults take 1 stick, **kids 4–12 take half a stick**. Post-workout is **adults only**.
- **One stick targets GLP-1 users: Morning.**
- **Pre-workout is caffeine-free.** No caffeine anywhere in the range.
- **No L-theanine** (Wilder's ingredient, and parents avoid it), **no melatonin** (a drug in Thailand), no NMN/resveratrol/fisetin/spermidine (unproven, not for kids, likely novel-food problems in Thailand).
- **Zero calorie** (< 5 kcal/stick) wherever possible. Pre-workout is the one exception to decide (§4).
- **Magnesium as bisglycinate** (OEM R&D). The OEM's odour limit is still open and decides the magnesium levels.
- **Positioning:** Wilder, LMNT, Liquid IV and the Bali brands all sell to the same young athletic buyer. This range is for **everyone else**: adults who want a simple daily protocol, with kids able to share.

## 3. Why each stick is built this way

**Morning (GLP-1 hero).** People on semaglutide or tirzepatide eat less, feel less thirsty and often get constipated.
- The stick replaces the morning salt water Huberman recommends.
- It adds the micronutrients a smaller plate misses (D3 + K2, B-vitamins, C).
- Magnesium helps regularity. Ginger and pomelo suit a queasy stomach (as flavour only; no nausea claim).
- Taurine: Sinclair 2 g, Johnson 1.5 g; promising metabolic and longevity data.
- **Message:** "Eating less? Don't drink less." / "The morning stick for smaller appetites." It's still a normal morning stick for everyone else.

**Pre-workout (caffeine-free pump).** Three things that work without stimulants:
- **sodium** (the "salt pump": more blood volume, better sweat replacement)
- **citrulline** (raises nitric oxide; watermelon is where citrulline was first found)
- **betaine** (Sinclair's TMG; ~2.5 g/day studied for power output)

Taurine for endurance. Safe for evening trainers, the caffeine-sensitive and teens.

**Post-workout (adults only).** 5 g creatine (Patrick's dose, the best-evidenced supplement there is). Taurine and vitamin C follow Johnson's Longevity Mix. Electrolytes for sweat lost. "No loading, no bloat" covers women's water-retention worry.

**Sleep.** Huberman's stack without theanine:
- **Magnesium bisglycinate:** the bisglycinate also supplies ~0.8 g glycine, so no separate glycine and its calories.
- **Apigenin 50 mg:** the chamomile flavonoid, plus some chamomile extract for the flavour story.
- **Myo-inositol 900 mg.**

Least sodium and acid of the four, in a small glass so nobody wakes up to pee.

## 4. Decisions still needed

1. **Pre-workout calories.** 2 g citrulline adds ~8 kcal, so the stick lands at ~11 kcal: low-calorie, not zero.
   - Accept the exception: "under 15 kcal".
   - Or drop citrulline and keep sodium + betaine + taurine (zero calorie, weaker pump).
   - A "real" pump dose is 6 g citrulline (~24 kcal, and it won't fit the stick with the salts).
2. **4th flavour and the post-workout name.** Pineapple has the most fitting story (and was liked earlier). Name: Swole Mate, or a non-sexual pun (e.g. *Muscle Memory*, *Pump & Pine*).
3. **The OEM's magnesium limit.** Sleep needs 120 mg magnesium from bisglycinate. If the odour cap really is ~50 mg Mg, Sleep has a problem.
4. **Stick size.** Pre- and post-workout are at ~10–10.5 g, the practical stick limit. Ca-AKG (Johnson's 2 g) doesn't fit; leave it out.

## 5. Regulatory flags for the OEM (Thailand)

- **Each active must be allowed in the chosen category** (dry beverage No. 356 vs dietary supplement): taurine, betaine, L-citrulline, apigenin,
  myo-inositol, ginger and chamomile extracts, vitamin D3/K2 fortification, creatine.
  Expect Post-workout (creatine) and possibly Sleep (apigenin) to need the supplement route.
- **Kids' dosing on pack.** A children's serving makes the products "for children" too, which brings stricter labelling and advertising rules. Ask the OEM what that triggers.
- **GLP-1 wording.** These are prescription drugs. Speak to the situation ("smaller appetite", "eating less"), not the drug or its side effects.
  The clinic/telemedicine channel can carry the drug-specific message.
- **Taurine without caffeine** should avoid the energy-drink rules. Confirm.
