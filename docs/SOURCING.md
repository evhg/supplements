## 1. Verdict on the quote: USD 0.25/stick all-in, 30k MOQ

**Fair to slightly high, not outrageous.** Halving it with the same quality and a lower MOQ is unrealistic in Thailand.

- **Our quote:** USD 0.25 is ≈ ฿8.4 per stick, including the printed stick, the retail box and Thai FDA registration.
- **What it costs to make** (bottom-up): **≈ USD 0.06–0.15 per stick (mid ~0.09).**
  - Ingredients for our complete-electrolyte formula: only USD 0.02–0.055. Mg bisglycinate and natural flavour are the biggest items.
  - The rest is filling labour (~35–40% of COGS) and film plus box (~20%).
- **One-off launch costs in Thailand:** ≈ USD 1,000–2,400. That covers FDA filing (฿2,000 government fee + ฿10–30k via an agent), nutrition lab test (฿6.7–12k) and gravure print cylinders (USD 480–800; digital print avoids these below ~20k sticks).
- **Thai small-brand OEM benchmarks** (implied from published MOQs and budgets):

  | OEM | Published terms | Implied cost per sachet |
  |---|---|---|
  | InnovaHealth | ฿70–80k at a 10k MOQ | ฿7–8 |
  | TNKG | ฿59k at a 5k MOQ | up to ~฿11.8 |

  The 50% target (≤ ฿4.2) is below what a Thai OEM can make for at this volume.
- **Fair price to negotiate toward:**
  - **First run:** ≈ USD 0.19–0.21, or keep 0.25 only if it includes cylinders, the lab test and FDA registration with no extras.
  - **Repeat orders:** ≈ USD 0.15–0.18, with the one-off costs itemised so they aren't charged again.
- **Biggest lever:** get the **30k MOQ to cover all 3 flavours** on one base blend (≈10k per flavour). The quote prices 30k per flavour, so this alone cuts the cash needed to launch 3 flavours from about USD 22.5k to USD 7.5k.

**Cost savers that keep quality:**
- **Magnesium:** blend bisglycinate 50:50 with magnesium citrate (USD 1.6–1.8/kg against 6–20).
- **Sweetener:** use Reb A, or Reb A with a little Reb M, instead of monk fruit MV50.
- **Flavours:** use the OEM's existing natural flavours rather than custom ones.
- **Box:** a standard size without lamination.
- **Volume:** commit to ~90k sticks a year, delivered in batches.

**Check before signing:**
- **Magnesium grade:** a "20% Mg" bisglycinate is usually a blend padded with magnesium oxide. Fully reacted bisglycinate is ~14% Mg.
- **Product category:** Thai FDA may classify this as an electrolyte beverage, a standards-controlled food, rather than a supplement. That decides the registration route and the composition rules.

## 3. Creatine raw material: Shaanxi Lanoric Biotech (Xi'an) COA review

Sample: "Creatine Powder", batch LX20260716, 800 kg, 200 mesh, made 16 Jul 2026, expires 15 Jul 2028.

**Fine:**
- **Assay** ≥ 99.9% (result 99.93%).
- **Heavy metals:** Pb ≤ 2, As ≤ 1, Cd ≤ 0.5, Hg ≤ 0.1 ppm.
- **Micro tests:** within limits, using Chinese GB methods.
- **Residual solvents:** not detected.

**Missing or questionable, so ask before buying:**
1. **No creatine-specific impurities.** Good creatine is judged on **creatinine, dicyandiamide (DCD), dihydrotriazine (DHT)** and ideally cyanide and sulfate. These are exactly the numbers Creapure and Pürest compete on, and none are on this certificate. Ask for them, ideally from a third-party lab (SGS, Eurofins, Intertek).
2. **Assay method not stated.** It should be HPLC.
3. **Loss on drying 5.98% (limit ≤ 7%) is unusual for creatine monohydrate.** The water of crystallisation alone is ~12% by weight, and pharmacopoeia-style specs are typically ~11.5–12.5% at 105 °C. Ask which method and temperature were used. It could mean a different test, or a partly dehydrated product.
4. **Who made it?** The address is an office tower in the Xi'an development zone, and the contact is an Alibaba storefront. Many Xi'an "biotech" companies are traders. Ask for the **actual manufacturer's name**, its GMP/ISO 22000/FSSC certificates, and halal/kosher if relevant.
5. **Internal certificate only.** It's signed and stamped by their own QC, with no third-party report.

**On mesh size** (the supplier's point is right):
- "Micronised" in the market usually means **≈ 200 mesh (≤ 74 µm)**. Standard creatine is ~80 mesh.
- 500 mesh (≤ 25 µm) is extra-fine. It flows badly, is hard to dose into sticks, and wastes material.
- **200 mesh is the right choice for our stick.** Before printing "micronised" on pack, check it with the OEM and Thai FDA, or say "fine-milled" instead.

**Commercial angle:** buying creatine ourselves and supplying it to the Thai OEM for filling works, but then **we** carry the quality risk on the raw material. That's another reason to get the full impurity panel. Compare the price against Creapure and Pürest quoted through the OEM.
