## 1. Shared base: 6 electrolytes

All three sticks carry **sodium, chloride, potassium, magnesium, calcium and phosphate**, at different levels per time of day.

**Ingredients:**
- **Salts:** sodium chloride (ideally partly Thai sea salt), trisodium citrate, potassium citrate, dipotassium phosphate, calcium lactate.
- **Magnesium: magnesium bisglycinate**, on the OEM R&D's advice (2026-11-11). Citrate avoids the fishy/metallic note but tastes bitter and absorbs moisture, which makes the powder clump. **Open:** the OEM earlier said ~50 mg bisglycinate is the most before odour and taste suffer. Confirm the maximum *elemental* magnesium per stick from bisglycinate, because that caps every stick's magnesium.
- **Taste:** citric/malic acid, natural flavours (carrier-free or non-caloric carrier), stevia (Reb M/Reb A) and/or monk fruit.
- **Colour:** natural colour only (beta-carotene or colouring foods).
- **Anti-caking:** silica only if needed.
- **None of these:** sugar, fruit powder, juice solids, honey, caffeine, L-theanine, artificial sweeteners, colours or flavours.

Vitamin targets for Afternoon Delight, ~100% of the Thai RDI each (verify with the OEM): vitamin C 60 mg, B1 1.5 mg,
B2 1.7 mg, niacin 20 mg, B6 2 mg, B12 2 µg.

**Why Wilder weighs 10.9 g:** 1,000 mg sodium mostly as sodium citrate is ~4.3 g on its own. Add potassium citrate
~1.7 g and magnesium ~0.7 g, and that's ~6.6 g of salts; the remaining ~4.3 g is acid, flavour, sweetener and probably
a bulking agent. Stick weight reflects the salt form and fillers, not the amount of minerals. Afternoon Delight
carries more total electrolytes (1,915 mg) than Wilder in 7.2 g.

**Calorie note (v1.3):** glycine in bisglycinate adds ~2.4–3.4 kcal per stick, so all three now sit at ~4–5 kcal, right at the zero-calorie limit. Afternoon Delight's acid dropped to 0.7 g to stay under. If the OEM's calculation lands above the Thai threshold, cut magnesium or acid further. Glycine in bisglycinate is the main calorie source.

## 3. Creatine spec (Morning Glory)

- **Grade:** creatine monohydrate, ≥ 99.9%, **200 mesh** ("micronised" in market terms; not 500 mesh, which fills badly).
- **Lab tests:** batch certificate of analysis that includes **creatinine ≤ 100 ppm, dicyandiamide ≤ 50 ppm, dihydrotriazine ≤ 3–5 ppm**, cyanide and heavy metals,
  with a third-party lab report.
- **Source:** Creapure® (preferred for label value) vs Pürest™ vs the Chinese supply reviewed in `SOURCING.md` §3. Decide on price and documents.
- **Mixing:** must mix without gritty residue in 400–500 ml after ~20 s of shaking.

## 4. Questions the OEM must answer before we lock the formulas

1. **Registration route per stick.** Wilder is a dry beverage under MOPH No. 356, and the plain base fits that route.
   - **Creatine:** may need the dietary-supplement route (and its rules on warnings and allowed doses).
   - **Added vitamins:** may need fortification approval or the supplement route.
   - **Botanical extracts:** must be on the Thai list of plants permitted in food.

   Ideally all three use one route. If not, what does each cost and how long does it take?
2. **Allowed claims and wording** on each route: "electrolytes", "creatine", "vitamins", "evening"/"wind down", "caffeine-free", "zero calorie".
3. **Taste trials:**
   - magnesium citrate:bisglycinate ratio
   - creatine bitterness under pomelo
   - vitamin B off-taste under watermelon
   - botanical bitterness under peach
4. **Calorie calculation** for each stick against the Thai energy-free rule.
5. **MOQ split:** 10k sticks per stick type within 30k, plus price per stick for each (Morning Glory will cost more).
6. **"Nothing artificial":** written confirmation for the flavours, sweeteners and colours.
